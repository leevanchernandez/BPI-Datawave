# Challenge log

Each row is an attack on the idea or the code, and what changed. Newest at the bottom.

| Attack | Evidence | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-learning rule suggestions are the headline | BSO ranked inquiries and status above extra documents, and said extras are handled by phone. A simulation only recovered planted rules, by construction. | Self-learning demoted to an optional BSO-side suggestion. Headline is the counter check, auto status and the resubmission reconciler. |
| Near-spelling surnames (Santos vs Santoz) pass an OCR-tolerant name match | Experiment: name-mismatch recall 0.85 to 0.94 | Fuzzy matching is earned by low OCR confidence only. Both fields read confidently: strict compare. |
| OCR noise causes false alarms | Experiment: 26 percent false alarms at 15 percent field noise, 66 percent at 50 percent | OCR folding (rn/m, 0/o, l/1/i), lost-space handling, confidence gate. Now 5 percent at 15 percent noise, 23 percent at 50 percent. Residue comes from silent high-confidence misreads, which cannot be fixed by the checker. |
| "Complete" can hide uncertainty | A low-confidence mismatch became a review flag, but complete stayed true | `complete` now requires no defects and no review flags. False-complete is 0 percent in the simulation at every noise level. |
| A bigger AI role makes the system less trustworthy | A hallucinated field or wrong status answer is worse than the email loop | Models only read, draft and answer from tool results. Outputs are schema-validated, doc types are an enum, unreadable pages flag review, the agent has a turn cap and hands off to the BSO. |
| The simulation measures its own generator | Same assumptions in generator and checker | The experiment is labeled simulated OCR noise. It says nothing about Textract or any model. Real measurement needs AWS access and the 60-document test. |

| `complete=true` could be produced by many routes | Adversarial code review reproduced: a consistent packet for a different client passed; garbage dates passed; Jr vs Sr and Maria vs Jose compared equal; low OCR confidence made name matching more lenient; `pageTotal` of 5 million took 4.6 s and 1.8 GB; a blank civil status skipped the spouse fields | Checker rebuilt to fail closed. Packet is compared to the referral. Missing names, dates and civil status become review items. Dates are really parsed and compared by Manila calendar day. Name compare keeps Maria, Jr and Sr, and never grants a match on spelling similarity. Page numbers are clamped. Every repro is now a regression test. |
| The model could be talked into anything | Text printed on a page reaches the model. It could invent an expiry or a name. | Any name or date the model returns must be visible in the OCR text of the same page, or it becomes a review item. Drafted messages that sound like approval are discarded for a template. |
| Trust boundary was open | Caller supplied the referral; any caller could read any case's uploads or ask about any referral; DynamoDB would have rejected identity-mismatch defects (undefined values) | Upload keys must sit under the referral's own prefix. `/ask` tools are bound to one referral and an unknown referral is reported as unknown, not as nothing lacking. Marshalling fixed and tested. Still open: no authorizer, and the referral must come from a trusted store in production. |
| The experiment was circular | Injected mismatches were read at a fixed 0.97 confidence, so the strict path always caught them. Recall was 1.00 by construction. | Injected-mismatch confidence now varies. The table separates "found for the right reason" from "flagged for any reason", gives sample sizes and intervals, and asserts more than the zero-noise row. The honest cost of failing closed is a high review rate under noise. |
